Overview

A t-test compares the averages of two groups. A one-way between-subjects ANOVA does the same job when you have three or more groups: it asks whether at least one group's average differs from the others. If it does, follow-up comparisons tell you which groups differ.

Our example is about social media and conspiracy beliefs. Research suggests that people who get their news from social media, and who use it more often, express more conspiracy beliefs (Enders et al., 2023); that passive social media use increases belief in conspiracy theories (Gkinopoulos et al., 2023); and that some platforms encourage conspiracy beliefs while others discourage them (Theocharis et al., 2021).

Using our class's data, we'll test whether the platform people use most is associated with their conspiracy beliefs. In the online survey, participants answered the question “What is your most used social media platform?” The three most common responses were Instagram, Snapchat, and TikTok, so those are the three groups we'll compare.

Learning objectives — by the end of this lab you should be able to:

  • Identify the independent variable (factor), its levels, and the dependent variable in a one-way ANOVA
  • Run a one-way between-subjects ANOVA with Tukey's HSD follow-up comparisons in jamovi
  • Check and interpret the assumptions of normality (Shapiro-Wilk) and homogeneity of variance (Levene's)
  • Decide whether the ANOVA is significant, and use the follow-up comparisons to say which groups differ and how
  • Write a complete, correctly formatted APA-style results sentence

Part 2

Running a one-way between-subjects ANOVA

Let's walk through conspiracy beliefs together.

  1. Make sure you're in the Analyses tab.
  2. In the ribbon, click ANOVA → One-Way ANOVA. The Analysis and Output windows should open.
  3. Move the dependent variable (ConspiracyBeliefs) to the Dependent Variables box. Move the independent variable (SocMedSite) to the Grouping Variable box.
  4. From the options in the Analysis window:
    • Under Variances, check Assume equal (Fisher's) and uncheck Don't assume equal (Welch's).
    • Under Additional Statistics, check Descriptives table.
    • Under Assumption Checks, check Homogeneity test and Normality test.
  5. Open the Post-Hoc Tests drop-down menu:
    • Select Tukey (equal variances).
    • Under Statistics, check Flag significant comparisons in addition to the default options.
  6. Your output is ready!

Reminder: you don't need to capture a screenshot for this conspiracy beliefs example. For neuroticism in Part 6 and procrastination in Part 7, you will — so once your output is ready, copy or screenshot the entire output (including the follow-up comparisons) before moving on.

In this lab, you'll run the follow-up comparisons whether or not the overall ANOVA turns out to be significant.

Here's what you should get:

This is the ANOVA table and the group descriptives. The ANOVA gives F(2, 186) = 2.46, p = .088. Since p > .05, we fail to reject the null hypothesis: there are no statistically significant differences in conspiracy beliefs between the three platforms.

Both assumptions are met: the Shapiro-Wilk p (.654) and Levene's p (.990) are both greater than .05.

These are the follow-up comparisons. None of the three is significant (all p > .05): TikTok vs. Snapchat p = .222, TikTok vs. Instagram p = .141, and Snapchat vs. Instagram p = .989. (We run them regardless of whether the ANOVA is significant.)

Model APA sentence: "A one-way between-subjects ANOVA indicated that there were not statistically significant differences between the three groups in conspiracy beliefs, F(2, 186) = 2.46, p = .09."

Part 3

Understanding the output

Your output has several pieces. Here's what each one tells you (jamovi may arrange them in a different order than we discuss them here).

Group descriptives

This tells you about your sample: for each group, the number of participants (N), the mean, and the standard deviation (SD).

Assumption checks

  • Normality test (Shapiro-Wilk) — if p < .05, we violate the assumption of normality. If p > .05, we meet it.
  • Homogeneity of variance test (Levene's) — if p < .05, we violate the assumption of homogeneity of variance (HOV). If p > .05, we meet it.

The ANOVA results

  • F — your observed F value (Fobs).
  • df1 — the degrees of freedom in the numerator (dfbetween).
  • df2 — the degrees of freedom in the denominator (dfwithin).
  • p — the p-value. If p < .05, we reject the null hypothesis that all the group means are equal: at least one group differs from the others. If p > .05, we fail to reject the null: we have no evidence that the groups differ.

Several different p-values. Your output has p-values in the Shapiro-Wilk table, in Levene's table, in the ANOVA results, and in the follow-up comparisons. The first two check assumptions. Only the ANOVA p answers “is there at least one difference?”, and the follow-up p-values tell you which pairs of groups differ.

Follow-up comparisons (Tukey's HSD)

The ANOVA tells us whether there's a difference somewhere, but not where. The post hoc tests compare every pair of groups. With three groups, that's three pairwise comparisons:

  • TikTok vs. Snapchat
  • TikTok vs. Instagram
  • Snapchat vs. Instagram

(Tukey's HSD adjusts for the fact that we're making three comparisons at once.) The results are laid out as a grid: find the spot where one group's row meets another group's column, and you'll see the comparison between those two groups. For each comparison, jamovi reports a mean difference (Row − Column) and a p-value. If p < .05, the comparison is significant, meaning those two group means do differ from one another — and the sign of the mean difference tells you which group is higher (a positive value means the group in the row scored higher than the group in the column). Because we checked “Flag significant comparisons,” jamovi will mark the significant ones for you.

Part 4

Writing an APA-formatted sentence

To write the APA-formatted sentence for a one-way between-subjects ANOVA, you need:

  • The observed F value
  • The degrees of freedom (dfbetween and dfwithin)
  • The p-value

Remember: statistical symbols (F, p) are always italicized. If jamovi reports p < .001, write p < .001.

The sentence follows this template — choose “were” if the ANOVA was significant (p < .05) and “were not” if it wasn't:

"A one-way between-subjects ANOVA indicated that there [were / were not] statistically significant differences between the three groups in [dependent variable], F(dfbetween, dfwithin) = ___, p = ___."
