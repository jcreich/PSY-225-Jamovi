Overview

So far we've described variables one at a time — their center, their spread, their shape. This lab introduces z-scores: a way of re-expressing any raw score in terms of how many standard deviations it sits above or below the mean. Once a score is a z-score, we can use the standard normal (z) table to say something very precise: what percentage of scores fall above it, below it, or between two of them.

Learning objectives — by the end of this lab you should be able to:

  • Create a computed variable in jamovi that converts raw scores into z-scores, using the Z() function
  • Interpret what a z-score's sign and magnitude tell you about a score
  • Use a z-table to find the percentage of scores above, below, or between given z-scores
  • Use z-scores to identify potential outliers

Have your z-table handy — either the one from your textbook, or a printed copy — you'll need it throughout this lab.

Part 2

Calculating z-scores in jamovi

As an example, let's calculate z-scores for IQScore.

  1. Make sure you're in the Data tab.
  2. Highlight the IQScore column (click on the column header).
  3. Click Add (in the ribbon, under Variables options) → Computed Variable → Insert.
  4. Name the new variable ZIQScore and give it the description: z-scores for IQScore.
  5. Click the fx dropdown menu, find the Z function, and double-click it. This should add the function after the equals sign.
  6. In the same fx dropdown, find IQScore and double-click it. This should add the variable inside the function's parentheses.
  7. Click outside the function box to populate the z-scores in the ZIQScore column.

Part 3

ZIQScore — what is it?

The values in the ZIQScore column are the corresponding z-scores of the values in the IQScore column. For example, participant P017 has an IQScore of 80, which is associated with a z-score of about −1.16.

Because that z-score is negative, we know an IQScore of 80 is below average. The size of the number (1.16) tells us how far below average it is — about 1.16 standard deviations below the mean.

Part 4

Take a closer look at the z-scores

To aid our understanding, let's get the descriptive statistics and a histogram of the ZIQScore column.

  1. Go to the Analyses tab.
  2. Click Exploration → Descriptives.
  3. Move ZIQScore into the Variables box.
  4. Under Plots, check Histogram.

Part 5

Using z-scores to spot potential outliers

Once your data is standardized, spotting a potential outlier is simple: a common rule of thumb is that any case with a z-score beyond ±3 (that is, more than 3 standard deviations from the mean) is worth a closer look.
