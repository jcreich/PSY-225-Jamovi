Overview

In the last lab, we used a correlation to ask whether two variables go together. Simple linear regression goes one step further: it lets us use one variable (the predictor, or IV) to predict another (the outcome, or DV), and tells us how well that prediction works.

Previous research suggests that the Dark Triad traits are positively associated with conspiracy ideation (Kay, 2021). However, other research indicates that only Machiavellianism and psychopathy are associated with conspiracy beliefs, finding little or no association with narcissism (Hughes & Machan, 2021; March & Springer, 2019).

Today, we will test whether the Dark Triad traits predict belief in conspiracy theories using our class data and simple linear regression.

Learning objectives — by the end of this lab you should be able to:

  • Run a simple linear regression in jamovi
  • Decide whether the overall regression model is statistically significant
  • Identify and interpret the intercept (B0) and slope (B1)
  • Interpret R2 as the proportion of variance explained
  • Write the regression equation and use it to make a prediction
  • Write complete, correctly formatted APA-style results sentences

Part 3

Understanding the output

Is the overall regression significant?

The Model Fit Measures table reports the Overall Model Test: an F value, two degrees of freedom (df1 and df2), and a p value. This is where we look to decide whether the overall regression is statistically significant.

  • Significant if p < .05
  • Non-significant if p > .05 — we fail to reject the null hypothesis, so the predictor does not significantly predict the outcome.

R2

The R2 value in the Model Fit Measures table is the proportion of variance in the DV explained by the IV. Multiply by 100 to get a percentage. For example, R2 = .05 means the predictor explains 5% of the variance in the outcome.

The model coefficients

The Model Coefficients table reports the two numbers we need for our prediction line:

  • Intercept (B0) = the value in the Estimate column for the intercept. It is the predicted score on the DV when the IV = 0.
  • Slope (B1) = the value in the Estimate column for the predictor. It tells us how much the DV changes for a one-unit increase in the IV. The slope is significant if p < .05.

The sign of the slope gives the direction: a positive slope means the DV goes up as the IV goes up, and a negative slope means the DV goes down as the IV goes up.

Writing and using a regression equation

The regression equation is

Ŷ = B0 + B1X

where Ŷ is the predicted score on the DV and X is a score on the IV. Replace B0 and B1 with the estimates from your Model Coefficients table to write the equation for your data. Then, to predict the DV for a particular person, replace X with their score on the IV and calculate Ŷ.

Part 4

Reporting a regression

For simple linear regression, you write two APA-formatted sentences.

Sentence 1: Overall regression

"The predictor did (or did not) significantly predict the outcome, F(df1, df2) = ___, p = ___, R2 = ___."

The p value determines whether you choose did or did not:

  • p < .05 → did
  • p > .05 → did not

Sentence 2: Slope (only if the overall regression is significant)

"Specifically, increases (or decreases) in the predictor predicted increases (or decreases) in the outcome, t = ___, p = ___."

The sign of the slope determines whether you choose increases or decreases:

  • Positive slope → increases
  • Negative slope → decreases

Remember: statistical symbols (F, t, p, R2) are always italicized. If jamovi reports p < .001, write p < .001.
