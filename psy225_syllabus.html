The big picture

Course Overview

Catalogue Description

An introduction to the statistical techniques most often used by behavioral scientists for descriptive purposes and hypothesis testing. Parametric techniques are emphasized, with nonparametric tests included.

Prerequisites: PSY 105 and MAT 111 (or a higher-level MAT course). Majors only, or permission of the instructor.

Class Objectives

This course builds a working foundation in the statistical practices most used in the behavioral sciences, covering descriptive and inferential statistics for univariate designs. The broader aim is to let you practice thinking like a psychologist — someone who relies on data to understand how people think, feel, and behave. By the end of the semester, you should be able to:

  • Appreciate the importance and everyday uses of statistics
  • Understand the logic and fundamentals of hypothesis testing
  • Perform basic statistical analyses, by hand and with software
  • Make inferences and evaluate statistics in both research and daily life
What you'll walk away with

Learning Outcomes & Program Objectives

  • Understand basic statistical concepts such as null hypothesis significance testing and effect size.
  • Identify the appropriate statistical analysis for a research question or dataset, and apply it.
  • Communicate and interpret statistical findings clearly, in writing and orally, using APA style.
  • Identify common statistical missteps and critically evaluate analyses used by others.
How class time works

Course Format

Learning happens through reading, lecture, in-class activities, and homework. Lecture sessions introduce new content paired with an in-class activity; lab sessions revisit that material and teach you to run the analyses in Jamovi, with time built in for activities and homework.

It doesn't matter whether you read the text before or after class — just that you read it.

This course is built around understanding, not memorization: assignments happen in small groups, and every exam is open-note. Both the instructor and TA are available outside class during office hours and by appointment.

Ground rules for the semester

Class Policies

This document is a contract

This syllabus describes the course, your responsibilities, and how you'll be evaluated — treat it as a contract between instructor and student. Registering beyond the add/drop deadline (January 20th) is an implied agreement to the terms stated here.

Copyright Use & Ownership

Lecture slides, recordings, handouts, and exam copies are the instructor's intellectual property under UNCW's Copyright Use and Ownership Policy. Redistributing them — including posting to websites — without permission is prohibited and may constitute infringement.

Attendance

Attendance and participation are required. You're responsible for everything covered in class, whether or not you attend — arrange notes from a classmate if you must miss a session.

Late Work — In-Class Activities & Labs Only

This policy does not apply to homework, which incurs a 50% penalty if late. In-class activities and labs are due at the end of the period assigned, but may be turned in up to one class period later for full credit. After that grace window, late work loses 50%. Everything must be submitted by the end of the unit in which it was assigned (i.e., by the unit exam or the last day of class) — after that, it scores zero.

Absent the day something is assigned? Email the instructor as soon as possible to request a copy so you can complete it during your absence.

Missed Quizzes & Exams

To arrange a make-up, email the instructor within 24 hours of the missed assessment. Make-ups must be completed within one week of the original date unless other arrangements are approved in advance — otherwise the score is zero. If you know in advance you'll be absent, notify the instructor ahead of time.

Statement on Wilmington 1898

In 1898, an estimated 60–300 unarmed Black residents of Wilmington were killed by white supremacists in a planned coup following a legal election, part of a broader campaign that drove Black political participation in the city and state into a long decline. Many who fled never returned. As members of the UNCW and Wilmington community, acknowledging this history is part of understanding our present. For further reading, see David Zucchino's Wilmington's Lie.
