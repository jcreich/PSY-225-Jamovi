Overview

Last time, we focused on opening data and understanding what a dataset looks like. This lab moves into your first real statistics: central tendency (mean, median, mode) and variability (range, variance, standard deviation) — the numbers that describe what a typical score looks like, and how spread out scores are around it.

Learning objectives — by the end of this lab you should be able to:

  • Use jamovi's Exploration → Descriptives tool to calculate mean, median, mode, variance, standard deviation, and range
  • Build a frequency table for a categorical variable
  • Identify a variable's level of measurement and use it to decide which descriptive statistics are actually appropriate — rather than calculating everything for every variable
  • Read and describe a histogram and box plot in plain language
  • Write a complete-sentence interpretation that refers explicitly to the statistics you calculated
  • Identify potential outliers using the mean ± 3×SD rule

Part 2

Calculating descriptive statistics: worked examples

Example A: a continuous (interval/ratio) variable

Let's walk through ReactionTimeMS together (reaction time in milliseconds; lower scores mean a faster reaction). Because this variable is continuous, every descriptive statistic below is meaningful for it — that won't be true for every variable, as you'll see in a moment.

  1. Step 1: Go to the Analyses tab.
  2. Step 2: Click Exploration. Select Descriptives from the options.
  3. Step 3: Move ReactionTimeMS from the box on the left to the box labeled Variables on the right.
  4. Step 4: Under Statistics, check Mode, Variance, and Range.
  5. Step 5: Under Plots, check Histogram and Box Plot.

Here's what you should get:

N60

Mean423.52
Median425.00
Mode404.00
Standard deviation57.87
Variance3348.46

Minimum257
Maximum563

When you interpret a variable, don't stop at the numbers — also look at the shape of the histogram. Is it roughly symmetric (one peak in the middle), skewed (a longer tail on one side), or does it have more than one peak (bimodal/multimodal)? This is part of what a complete interpretation includes.

"Students' reaction times cluster fairly close together, with a mean of 423.52ms and a median of 425.00ms — very close to each other, which is a good sign the distribution isn't heavily skewed. The histogram confirms this: it's roughly symmetric with a single peak, just slightly pulled toward faster times. Scores differ from the mean by about 57.87ms on average (the standard deviation), and the full range spans 306ms (from 257 to 563) — indicating a moderate, fairly typical amount of variability for a reaction-time measure."

Example B: building a frequency table for a categorical variable

Descriptive statistics like the mean and standard deviation don't make sense for every variable. For a categorical variable like Handedness, what we actually want is a frequency table — a count of how many cases fall into each category.

  1. Step 1: Go to the Analyses tab. Click Exploration. Select Descriptives.
  2. Step 2: Move Handedness into the Variables box.
  3. Step 3: Under Statistics, check only Mode — mean, median, variance, and standard deviation don't apply to a nominal variable like this one, since its categories don't have a meaningful numeric order.
  4. Step 4: Expand the Frequency Tables section and check the box to display a frequency table.

Here's what you should get:

HandednessCounts% of TotalCumulative %
Right5185.0%85.0%
Left813.3%98.3%
Ambidextrous11.7%100.0%

"The large majority of students in this sample are right-handed (85.0%), with a smaller group of left-handed students (13.3%) and only one ambidextrous student (1.7%). Since Handedness is nominal, the mode — Right — is the only measure of central tendency that applies here, and the frequency table is what lets us describe the variable's distribution."

Which statistics go with which level of measurement?

Use this as your reference for Part 3 below. Before calculating anything for a variable, decide what level of measurement it is first — that tells you which of these rows applies.

Level of measurementCentral tendencyVariabilityOther
NominalMode only—Frequency table
OrdinalMedian, ModeRange, Minimum, MaximumFrequency table (optional)
Interval / Ratio (continuous)Mean, Median, ModeStandard deviation, Variance, Range, Minimum, MaximumHistogram, Box plot

Notice that the mean and standard deviation only show up in the bottom row. If a variable is nominal or ordinal, leave those unchecked in jamovi — including them doesn't make sense mathematically, even if jamovi lets you check the box.
